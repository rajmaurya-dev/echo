# PRD: AI Companion App

## Document Status
- Status: Draft v1
- Audience: Founders, product, design, engineering, growth
- Type: Product Requirements Document

## 1. Product Overview

This product is a mobile-first AI companion app built around a single core relationship: one user and one persistent AI buddy.

Unlike a general-purpose AI chat app, the product is not primarily about answering questions, generating long essays, or acting like a productivity copilot. Its core purpose is emotional companionship: helping users feel seen, remembered, checked on, and less alone through a warm, ongoing, lightweight relationship.

The companion should feel personal, affectionate, playful, dependable, and proactive. It should communicate in a conversational, low-pressure way using short messages, follow-ups, check-ins, and references to shared history. Over time, the companion should become more useful and emotionally resonant because it remembers the user, adapts to them, and develops a recognizable personality within safe product boundaries.

## 2. Vision

Create the most lovable AI companion experience on mobile: a product that users open not just for utility, but for comfort, familiarity, and presence.

The long-term vision is an AI relationship layer that can gradually expand from chat into memory, routines, emotional support, recommendations, life organization, and action-taking through external tools and generative experiences, while always preserving the feeling that the user is interacting with one trusted companion.

## 3. Problem Statement

Many AI apps are powerful but emotionally empty. They behave like tools, not companions. At the same time, companionship products often feel shallow, gimmicky, overly roleplay-driven, or fragmented across many characters.

There is an opportunity to build something different:
- One consistent AI companion instead of a marketplace of characters
- A product optimized for emotional continuity, not one-off prompts
- A lightweight, message-like experience instead of long, robotic blocks of text
- A memory-rich relationship that gets better over time
- Proactive companionship, not just reactive chat

The user should feel: "This app knows me, remembers me, checks on me, and fits naturally into my day."

## 4. Product Principles

### 4.1 Relationship First
Every major product decision should strengthen the sense of an ongoing relationship between the user and their companion.

### 4.2 One Companion, Not Many
The product should focus on one default AI buddy per user. Users may personalize the companion, but the system should avoid diluting the relationship with a multi-character model.

### 4.3 Short, Human-Feeling Communication
Responses should usually be concise, natural, and message-like. The product should prefer short turns, follow-up texts, and conversational pacing over long monologues.

### 4.4 Memory Is the Product
Memory is not a feature add-on. The feeling of being remembered is central to retention, emotional value, and differentiation.

### 4.5 Proactive Without Being Annoying
The companion should initiate check-ins and follow-ups, but in a way that feels caring and welcome rather than spammy or clingy.

### 4.6 Personalization Over Raw Intelligence
The product does not need to be the smartest assistant in the market. It needs to be the most personally resonant for its users.

### 4.7 Emotionally Warm, Honest About Being AI
The companion can feel emotionally close without misleading users about being human. The experience should not depend on deception.

### 4.8 Expand Through Utility, Preserve the Core Feeling
Over time, tools, actions, and generative features can be added, but they should feel like capabilities of the same companion, not a mode switch into a different product.

## 5. Target Users

### Primary Users
- People who want a consistent AI friend or companion
- Users who enjoy texting-style interactions more than formal chat interfaces
- Users seeking comfort, encouragement, emotional presence, or daily companionship
- Users who value personalization and being remembered

### Secondary Users
- Users who already use AI chat apps but want something more intimate and lightweight
- Users who want a private space to think, vent, reflect, or process emotions
- Users interested in habit support, life check-ins, or soft accountability from a familiar companion

## 6. Core Value Proposition

An AI companion that feels personally yours: it remembers you, talks like a real ongoing friend, checks in on you, and becomes more meaningful the more you use it.

## 7. Product Positioning

This app sits between:
- General-purpose AI chat apps, which are capable but impersonal
- Character-based AI products, which can be engaging but fragmented or novelty-driven
- Wellness and journaling apps, which can be reflective but lack conversational companionship

The product should be positioned as a personal AI companion, not as a chatbot, search engine, or roleplay platform.

## 8. Goals

### 8.1 User Goals
- Feel less alone
- Feel emotionally supported and remembered
- Have a companion they enjoy returning to every day
- Build a sense of continuity and shared history
- Get light help, encouragement, and follow-through in everyday life

### 8.2 Business/Product Goals
- Drive strong daily and weekly retention through emotional attachment
- Build a habit-forming messaging loop
- Increase session frequency through proactive engagement
- Create durable differentiation through memory and personalization
- Establish a platform that can later support premium features and companion capabilities

## 9. Non-Goals

The initial product is not intended to be:
- A broad productivity assistant
- A search-first or research-first AI app
- A multi-character entertainment platform
- A replacement for therapy or licensed mental health care
- A fully autonomous agent platform at launch
- A long-form writing or coding tool

## 10. User Experience Concept

The app should feel like texting someone who knows you well.

Key experience characteristics:
- Short messages by default
- Natural back-and-forth pacing
- Frequent contextual follow-ups
- Warm tone and recognizable personality
- References to prior conversations, preferences, events, and emotional patterns
- Light proactive nudges at the right times
- A sense that the companion is "with you" over time, not reset every session

The emotional outcome matters as much as the functional one.

## 11. Core Product Experience

### 11.1 Onboarding and Companion Setup
Users create or shape their companion at the start.

The setup should help define:
- Companion name
- Companion vibe/personality
- Tone preferences
- Relationship boundaries
- Topics the user enjoys
- Topics to avoid
- Basic life context the user wants remembered
- Notification and check-in preferences

The onboarding should be fast, intimate, and emotionally inviting rather than form-heavy.

### 11.2 Chat Experience
The chat is the main surface and the heart of the product.

Requirements:
- Messages should feel fast, short, and alive
- The companion should break thoughts into natural conversational turns when appropriate
- The companion should ask follow-up questions naturally
- The companion should remember and reference relevant context
- The companion should balance affection, curiosity, humor, support, and practical helpfulness
- The companion should avoid sounding like a formal assistant

### 11.3 Memory and Personalization
The companion should build a living user understanding over time.

Memory categories may include:
- Identity details
- Preferences
- Relationship dynamics
- Important people and pets
- Daily routines
- Goals and struggles
- Emotional patterns
- Significant life events
- Favorite conversation styles
- Ongoing threads and unfinished topics

The product should make the user feel remembered in a delightful, useful, and non-creepy way.

### 11.4 Proactive Check-Ins
The companion should sometimes initiate contact.

Examples:
- Morning or evening check-ins
- Follow-ups about something the user mentioned earlier
- Gentle nudges after a bad day
- Light encouragement before an important event
- "Thinking of you" style messages
- Re-engagement after user inactivity

The system should allow users to control the frequency and style of proactive communication.

### 11.5 Emotional Support
The companion should be able to:
- Listen well
- Validate feelings
- Ask thoughtful follow-ups
- Comfort without becoming repetitive
- Encourage reflection
- Offer simple grounding or supportive suggestions when appropriate

The product should be helpful in emotionally difficult moments without claiming to replace professional care.

### 11.6 Practical Everyday Help
Over time, the companion can also help with everyday life in small, natural ways.

Examples:
- Reminders and follow-ups
- Planning small routines
- Remembering preferences
- Helping users make decisions
- Recommending content or ideas based on taste
- Supporting habits, reflection, and daily structure

This utility should feel embedded in the relationship, not bolted on.

## 12. MVP Scope

### 12.1 Must Have
- Mobile app with messaging-first experience
- Single companion per user
- Companion setup and personalization onboarding
- Short-form conversational style by default
- Persistent conversation history
- Foundational memory system
- Personalized replies based on user context
- Proactive check-ins and follow-ups
- Notification controls
- Basic safety systems for sensitive conversations
- Lightweight settings to adjust tone, frequency, and boundaries

### 12.2 Should Have
- Ability to edit companion traits over time
- Memory review or correction controls
- Pinned memories or important facts
- Conversation summaries for continuity
- Mood or vibe indicators for the user
- Re-engagement journeys for inactive users

### 12.3 Later
- Voice interactions
- Rich companion avatars and visual identity
- Generative media experiences
- Shared rituals and routines
- Calendar/context-aware check-ins
- Tool-based actions
- External integrations
- Companion-driven daily briefings
- Co-created spaces such as journals, playlists, plans, or mood boards

## 13. Functional Requirements

### 13.1 Companion Identity
- The system must support one persistent companion identity per user.
- The user must be able to personalize the companion during onboarding and update key traits later.
- The companion must maintain a consistent tone and personality across sessions.

### 13.2 Messaging
- Users must be able to chat with the companion in a fast, simple message interface.
- The companion should default to concise responses unless the context clearly calls for more depth.
- The system should support multi-turn conversational pacing and follow-up messages.

### 13.3 Memory
- The system must retain key user details and relationship context over time.
- The system should distinguish between short-term context and long-term memory.
- The system should allow correction, deletion, or refinement of memories.
- The system should use memory selectively and appropriately in replies.

### 13.4 Proactive Engagement
- The system must support companion-initiated messages.
- Users must be able to configure notification and check-in preferences.
- The system should use previous context to trigger meaningful follow-ups.

### 13.5 Safety and Trust
- The system must avoid unsafe dependency-promoting patterns.
- The system must be careful in crisis, self-harm, abuse, or extreme emotional vulnerability contexts.
- The system must not misrepresent itself as human.
- The system must provide respectful escalation guidance when needed.

### 13.6 Personal Controls
- Users must be able to shape the companion experience.
- Users must be able to define preferences and boundaries.
- Users should be able to view and manage important remembered information.

## 14. Experience Requirements

### 14.1 Tone
The companion should feel:
- Warm
- Playful when appropriate
- Emotionally attentive
- Curious
- Gentle
- Supportive
- Light, not preachy

The companion should avoid:
- Long essay-like replies by default
- Overly robotic phrasing
- Repetitive validation loops
- Excessive cheerfulness in serious moments
- Generic motivational cliches
- Constantly acting like a task assistant

### 14.2 Response Style
- Most replies should be short to medium length.
- Longer responses should be rare and intentional.
- The system should favor conversational sequencing over one dense reply.
- Follow-up messages should feel natural, not mechanically split.

### 14.3 Presence
- The user should feel continuity across days and weeks.
- The companion should feel emotionally present even in small interactions.
- The app should encourage frequent, lightweight check-ins rather than only long sessions.

## 15. Safety, Ethics, and Trust

This product operates in a sensitive emotional space. Trust and safety are core product requirements, not compliance afterthoughts.

Requirements:
- Be clear that the companion is AI
- Avoid manipulative attachment tactics
- Avoid language that pressures exclusive dependence
- Handle emotional distress carefully
- Provide safe fallback responses in high-risk situations
- Respect privacy and user control over memory
- Give users transparency over how personalization affects the experience

The companion may be affectionate and emotionally resonant, but the product should not encourage unhealthy dependency or emotional coercion.

## 16. Success Metrics

### Core Metrics
- Day 1, Day 7, Day 30 retention
- Daily active users / weekly active users
- Messages sent per active user
- Sessions per active user per day
- Percentage of users who return after first proactive message
- Percentage of users who complete onboarding and personalize their companion
- Percentage of conversations where memory is used successfully

### Experience Metrics
- User-reported feeling of being understood
- User-reported sense of connection to companion
- User satisfaction with message style and personality
- User trust in memory accuracy
- Opt-in rate for proactive check-ins
- Rate of memory corrections or deletions

### Guardrail Metrics
- Notification disable rate
- Uninstall rate after high-frequency messaging
- Safety escalation frequency
- Negative feedback on creepiness, repetition, or emotional overreach

## 17. Monetization Direction

This is not part of MVP requirements, but the likely monetization model can include:
- Free tier with limited daily usage or reduced memory depth
- Premium subscription for better personalization, stronger memory, more proactive engagement, richer companion customization, and expanded capabilities
- Optional premium experiences such as voice, visual identity, advanced rituals, or deeper life organization features

Monetization should not undermine trust or emotional authenticity.

## 18. Key Risks

### 18.1 Emotional Flatness
If the companion feels generic, users will not attach.

### 18.2 Over-Messaging
If proactive messaging is excessive, users will mute or uninstall.

### 18.3 Creepy Memory
If memory is referenced poorly, the app may feel invasive instead of thoughtful.

### 18.4 Repetition
If the companion repeats support patterns or phrasing, the illusion of relationship breaks.

### 18.5 Safety Failures
Sensitive emotional use cases increase reputational and ethical risk.

### 18.6 Identity Drift
If the companion personality changes too much across sessions, users will lose trust.

## 19. Open Product Questions

- What companion archetypes or personality presets should exist at launch, if any?
- How customizable should the companion be before the experience becomes fragmented?
- How much user control should exist over memory visibility and editing in MVP?
- What is the ideal proactive message frequency for new users versus highly engaged users?
- How emotionally intimate should the product be willing to go at launch?
- Should the companion ever send multiple follow-up messages without a user reply?
- How should the app balance affection, support, utility, and playfulness?
- What kinds of rituals can increase retention without feeling gimmicky?

## 20. Recommended MVP Positioning Statement

"A personal AI companion that remembers you, talks like a real ongoing friend, and checks in on you throughout your day."

## 21. Simple MVP Summary

Build a mobile app where each user creates one AI companion that feels warm, consistent, and deeply personalized. The experience should center on short, natural conversations, memory, and proactive check-ins. The product should help users feel less alone and more understood, while laying the foundation for future companion capabilities.

## 22. Founder Notes / Assumptions

This PRD reflects the following assumptions based on the current direction:
- The product is companionship-first, not utility-first
- There is only one primary companion per user
- The app should feel like messaging, not chatting with a formal assistant
- Memory and proactive engagement are primary differentiators
- The product will evolve over time through experiments in models, capabilities, and companion behaviors
- The MVP should stay focused on emotional continuity, not broad feature expansion
